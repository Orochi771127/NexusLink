# Formal Evolution Silhouette Rules R2

> Owner correction: an evolved form must look like a new form, not merely the
> same animal scaled up, recolored or surrounded by more VFX. At the same time,
> it must remain recognizably the same individual and the same biological
> creature family.

## 1. Identity anchors that may not change

Every lineage must preserve at least these anchors across all three forms:

1. species identity and anatomical anchors — feline ears／muzzle／tail, avian
   beak／wings／talons, cervid ears／antlers／hooves and similar traits remain
   traceable. A controlled transition to a bipedal or anthropomorphic evolved
   form is allowed when the lineage design calls for it;
2. face family — eye color／shape, muzzle or beak, ear placement and baseline
   expression remain traceable to Stage 1;
3. primary palette and heart-core location;
4. signature marking or appendage — for example Blazetail's single flame tail,
   ThunderPup's crystal ridge, Goldenspark's attached gear tail;
5. one-companion continuity — anthropomorphic evolution may change stance and
   hand／foot use, but it may not erase the source species or replace the
   character with an unrelated human, monster or borrowed character design.

## 2. Required evolution difference

Each later stage must change the design structure, not just the rendering:

- Stage 2 must introduce at least three readable changes among body proportion,
  head／ear／crown silhouette, shoulder or torso silhouette, tail／wing／fin
  structure, signature garment or attached natural material, and stance.
- Stage 3 must introduce at least three additional readable changes beyond
  Stage 2 and resolve the lineage's strongest visual motif.
- Size increase, hue shift, glow intensity and floating VFX do not count as
  structural changes by themselves.
- The Stage 1 face, palette, heart-core and signature motif must still make the
  lineage recognizable without labels.
- A later stage may become an anthropomorphic guardian／fighter／mystic when
  that transition is intentional and visually inherited from Stage 1. It may
  not become an unrelated human, giant boss, armored mount or machinery shell.

## 4. Blazetail reference authority

- Owner image 1 is the Stage 2 appearance authority.
- Owner image 2 is the Stage 3 appearance authority.
- Both supplied forms are upright anthropomorphic fox forms. R2 must preserve
  that body plan instead of placing their clothing onto a four-foot fox.
- Do not redesign their clothing, palette or flame concept into a different
  idea. Translate the supplied pixel designs into high-resolution illustrated
  art while preserving their exact stage roles and bipedal silhouettes.
- Pixel blocks may become fur, cloth, embroidery, crystal and luminous circuit
  detail. The design itself must not be replaced.

## 5. Sprite-sheet readiness and cute-bold art direction

Except for Blazetail, whose supplied Owner references remain the appearance
authority, every Stage 2 and Stage 3 concept must already be practical to turn
into an eight-direction sprite sheet:

- keep a compact, cute and readable head-to-body ratio while allowing a more
  capable evolved stance;
- use bold complementary color blocks and one or two saturated luminous accents
  while preserving the Stage 1 primary-color anchor;
- keep arms, legs, wings, fins and the single canonical tail visually separable
  from the torso in front, side, rear and diagonal views;
- avoid loose ribbons, dense translucent fur, particle clouds, detached orbiting
  props and stacks of tiny ornaments that would obscure joints or multiply
  animation work;
- reserve the strongest detail for the face, heart-core and one signature
  silhouette motif; secondary surfaces should use animation-friendly shapes;
- cute does not mean infantile: large expressive eyes, rounded facial planes,
  readable ears and paws or talons may remain while Stage 2 and Stage 3 gain
  confidence, height and visual authority.

## 6. R2 review gate

An R2 board passes only when all three forms are fully inside frame, have valid
species anatomy, contain no detached required body part, and can be sorted into
Stage 1／2／3 without labels. Non-Blazetail later forms must also pass the
sprite-sheet-readiness rules above. R1 remains historical reference and is not
deleted.
